How to Import Hair Extensions from India to the USA

Quick overview: importing hair extensions from India

To import hair extensions from India into the United States, start by identifying the exact product and supplier, confirming product specifications, agreeing on shipping terms, checking current customs classification and import requirements, and calculating the full landed cost. A customs broker or qualified trade adviser can help confirm requirements for your shipment. Rules and tariff rates can change.

How to source or supply imported hair extensions

The query “how to supply import hair extensions” can describe either side of the transaction. US buyers need a reliable exporter, clear product specifications and a documented shipment. Indian suppliers need consistent product descriptions, batch photos, packing details, invoices and an agreed delivery method. Both parties should confirm who is responsible for export clearance, international shipping, customs entry, duties and final delivery.

Step 1: define the hair product before requesting quotes

Specify whether you need human hair or synthetic hair; loose bulk hair, wefts, bundles, closures, wigs or finished extensions; raw or processed hair; texture; length; color; bundle weight; and packaging. Different products may be classified differently for customs purposes. Do not treat all hair extensions as one interchangeable item.

Step 2: verify the Indian supplier

Request business details, recent product photos, sample availability, written quality specifications, references where appropriate and a clear policy for defects or mismatched goods. Test samples before placing a large order. Ask whether the hair has been colored, chemically treated or blended with other fibers.

Step 3: agree on price and shipping terms

Ask for a written quotation that states quantity, unit price, currency, lead time, packaging and Incoterms or other agreed shipping responsibilities. A low unit price may not be the lowest delivered price after freight, insurance, brokerage, duties and domestic delivery.

Step 4: prepare shipping and customs documents

Commercial invoices and packing lists are commonly needed, along with transportation documents and any additional paperwork applicable to the specific product and shipment. Confirm the required information with your freight forwarder and customs broker before dispatch.

Step 5: check the correct HS/HTS classification

Searches for “hs code for hair extensions” and “hs code for synthetic hair extension” show interest in classification. The appropriate US HTS code depends on what the goods actually are—for example, human hair versus synthetic fibers, and unworked hair versus prepared or finished articles. Do not rely on a single generic code from a blog post. Confirm classification against the current US Harmonized Tariff Schedule and with a licensed customs broker when needed.

Step 6: calculate landed cost

Estimate product cost + international freight + insurance + customs duties and applicable fees + broker charges + inland delivery + potential inspection or storage charges. Convert the result into cost per usable bundle or unit. This makes supplier quotes comparable and helps set realistic retail margins.

Step 7: inspect goods and plan repeat orders

Check quantities, lengths, colors, weights and packaging against the purchase order. Document discrepancies immediately. Keep shipment records and product specifications so later orders can be compared consistently.

Frequently asked questions

Can I import hair extensions from India to the USA? Generally, commercial importation is possible, subject to the requirements applicable to the exact product and shipment.

What is the HS code for hair extensions? It depends on composition and how the hair has been prepared or made into an article. Confirm the current code before shipping.

Are synthetic and human hair extensions classified the same way? Not necessarily.

How do I find a reliable supplier? Start with clear specifications, samples, documentation and small trial orders.

Official sources to verify before shipping

Consult the current US International Trade Commission Harmonized Tariff Schedule (hts.usitc.gov) and US Customs and Border Protection guidance (cbp.gov). These sources are for checking current requirements, not a substitute for shipment-specific advice.
