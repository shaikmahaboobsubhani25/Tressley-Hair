Step-by-Step Guide to Import Hair Extensions from India to the USA 

Step 1: Understand the HS Code and US Import Classification

Before you do anything else, know your product's classification code. Every item entering the USA is classified under the Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United States (HTSUS), which is the US-specific version of the international HS system.

Which HS Code Applies to Hair Extensions?

Human hair extensions from India fall under HS code 6703.00, which covers human hair that has been dressed, thinned, bleached, or otherwise worked. The specific HTSUS subheading is 6703.00.00.

Synthetic hair extensions use a different code: 6704.20 (wigs and articles of synthetic textile materials). If your supplier ships genuine raw human hair but your invoice lists the wrong code, CBP can test the fibers at the port and penalize you for misclassification.

Key point: India's GSP (Generalized System of Preferences) benefits were withdrawn in June 2019, but the MFN rate for HS 6703.00 is already 0%. So in practice, there is no customs duty impact for importing raw human hair from India to the USA today.

One Important Rule on Country of Origin

Even if your hair is sourced from Indian temples, if it was processed or substantially transformed in China before shipping to you, US customs may classify the country of origin as China. That affects tariff rates. Always confirm with your supplier that the hair was manufactured and exported directly from India, and make sure the commercial invoice states India as the country of origin.

Step 2: Find and Verify a Genuine Indian Manufacturer

This step is where most wholesale buyers run into problems. The Indian hair industry includes a mix of actual manufacturers, trading companies pretending to be manufacturers, and middlemen with polished websites. Buying from a trading company instead of a manufacturer means you pay 15 to 40% more and lose direct quality control.

How to Tell a Manufacturer from a Trading Company?

Ask for their IEC number. The IEC (Importer Exporter Code) is issued by India's Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT). Every legitimate Indian manufacturer who exports internationally holds one. Ask your prospective supplier to share their IEC. Then verify it at the DGFT public portal (). If they hesitate or share a number that does not match their business name, walk away.

Request a video walkthrough of the factory floor. Real manufacturers will show you production. They will walk you through washing, sorting, wefting, and packing on a live video call. If a supplier only shows you their showroom or a catalog of photos, that is a sign they are reselling someone else's product.

Check their active GSTIN. An active Goods and Services Tax Identification Number (GSTIN) should appear on every invoice. Cross-check the entity name on the GSTIN with the name on their IEC. They should match.

Step 3: Request Samples Before Committing to a Bulk Order

No reputable manufacturer will refuse a paid sample request. A sample order typically costs USD 300 to 800 and gives you a real bundle or weft to test before committing to a full wholesale shipment.

Use your sample to check three things:

• Do a burn test: hold a strand to a lighter. Human hair burns slowly, smells like burnt protein, and leaves ash you can crumble. Synthetic hair melts, smells like chemicals, and leaves a plastic bead.

• Run the slide test: slide your fingers from tip toward the root. If the hair resists in both directions equally, the cuticles are aligned. If it catches strongly going from tip to root, the cuticles are not aligned and the hair will tangle quickly after installation.

• Wash it once: real raw hair holds its texture after washing without conditioning treatments. Acid-coated or silicone-coated hair will feel rough and dry after the first wash.

If the sample passes all three tests, you have a real raw hair product worth ordering in bulk.

Step 4: Sort Out the Import Documentation

US CBP requires specific documents for every commercial shipment above USD 800. For shipments above USD 2,500, you also need a formal entry filing and a US Customs bond.

Documents Your Indian Supplier Must Provide

1. Commercial Invoice: Lists the supplier name, address, IEC, and GSTIN; buyer details; HS code 6703.00; quantity and weight; declared value in USD; and country of origin (India). Every field must match your packing list exactly.

2. Packing List: Shows weight per box, number of bundles, length breakdown, and texture details. CBP cross-checks this against the commercial invoice.

3. Airway Bill (AWB): Issued by DHL or FedEx when your shipment is collected from the factory. This is your shipment tracking document.

Ask your supplier to share the commercial invoice draft before the shipment is collected. That way you can catch discrepancies before CBP does.

What You Need on the US Side

5. An IRS Employer Identification Number (EIN) or your personal Social Security Number for the customs entry filing. Your EIN is your importer of record identifier.

6. A US Customs bond: Required for formal entries above USD 2,500. Your customs broker arranges this. A single-entry bond costs approximately USD 50 to 100 for most hair shipments.

7. A licensed US customs broker: Not legally required but strongly recommended for first-time importers. Brokers file the entry with CBP, handle duty payment, and deal with any queries. Fees typically range from USD 100 to 200 per shipment.

Step 6: Understand What Happens at US Customs

Here is what the import clearance process looks like from the moment your shipment lands in the USA:

8. Arrival and entry filing: Your shipment arrives at a US port of entry (commonly Miami, JFK, LAX, or Chicago O'Hare for air freight from India). Your customs broker files a CBP entry electronically before or at the time of arrival.

9. CBP review: CBP reviews the entry documents. For HS 6703.00 from India with a 0% duty rate, clearance is usually straightforward if documentation is complete and consistent.

10. Potential examination: CBP can select any shipment for physical inspection. This is more common for first-time importers. An examination adds 1 to 3 days to clearance.

11. Duty and fee payment: Even at 0% duty, you pay a Merchandise Processing Fee (MPF) of 0.3464% of the declared value, with a minimum of USD 31.67 and a maximum of USD 614.35 per entry. You also pay a Harbor Maintenance Fee (HMF) of 0.1250% on sea shipments (not applicable for air freight).

12. Release and delivery: Once cleared, DHL or FedEx delivers to your door within 1 to 2 business days.

Common Mistakes That Delay Clearance

• Mismatched values between the commercial invoice and packing list. CBP flags any inconsistency.

• Wrong HS code on the invoice. Synthetic hair codes and human hair codes are different. Make sure your supplier lists 6703.00 on the invoice.

• Missing IEC on the supplier invoice. CBP requires the exporter to be identifiable. No IEC means potential holds.

• Declaring a falsely low value to reduce fees. CBP has market price benchmarks for hair extensions and will query undervalued shipments.

Frequently Asked Questions

What HS code do I use to import raw hair extensions from India?

Use HS code 6703.00.00 for human hair extensions. This covers raw, dressed, or otherwise worked human hair. The US MFN duty rate for this code from India is 0% as of 2026. Always make sure your supplier lists this code on the commercial invoice.

Do I need a customs broker to import hair extensions from India?

You are not legally required to use a customs broker, but it is strongly recommended for first-time importers. A licensed broker files your CBP entry, arranges your customs bond, and handles any queries from customs officers. Broker fees typically range from USD 100 to 200 per shipment, which is far less than the cost of a delayed shipment.

How long does shipping from India to the USA take?

Air freight via DHL or FedEx takes 3 to 7 business days from manufacturer pickup to US door delivery. Customs clearance typically adds 1 to 3 business days. Verified Indian manufacturers like Tressley Hair ship from Andhra Pradesh via DHL and FedEx with full tracking.

What is the minimum order quantity for importing raw Indian hair wholesale?

Most verified Indian manufacturers start wholesale orders at 3 to 10 bundles, which equals approximately 300 to 1,000 grams of hair. Private label and custom orders typically require higher minimums. Ask your supplier to confirm the MOQ per SKU in writing before placing any order.

What is the difference between a manufacturer and a trading company in India?

A manufacturer produces the hair in-house, from sourcing temple donations through wefting and packing. A trading company buys from multiple factories and resells, adding a 15 to 40% markup. The key verification step is asking for the supplier's DGFT-issued IEC number and verifying it at the DGFT public portal.

Is raw Indian temple hair legal to import into the USA?

Yes. Human hair is not a controlled import in the USA. There are no FDA restrictions on importing human hair extensions. The only requirement is correct HS code classification and complete commercial documentation for CBP customs clearance.

Can I import hair extensions from India without paying duty?

For human hair under HS code 6703.00.00 imported from India, the US MFN duty rate is currently 0%. You will still pay a Merchandise Processing Fee of 0.3464% of the declared value (minimum USD 31.67) plus any courier handling charges, but there is no actual customs duty on the hair itself.
