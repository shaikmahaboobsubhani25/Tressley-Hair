Raw Indian Hair Colour Chart: What Colour Is Natural Indian Hair?

What color is Indian hair naturally?

Natural Indian hair is commonly dark brown to black, but there is no single color shared by everyone in India. Some people naturally have lighter brown or warmer-looking hair. Shade can vary between individuals and even between strands on the same head. Lighting, sun exposure and camera settings can also change how a bundle appears in photographs.

Natural Indian hair color chart

Use this practical visual guide when reviewing real hair photos: natural black can appear nearly black indoors; soft black can show subtle brown in sunlight; dark brown appears visibly brown in brighter light; medium or warm brown is possible but should not be assumed to be the natural shade of every raw-hair bundle. These are descriptive categories, not a standardized color-number chart.

Can Indians have naturally brown hair?

Yes. Natural hair color varies across people in India. Dark shades are common, but naturally brown hair exists. A seller should describe the specific bundle you are purchasing rather than suggesting that all Indian hair is identical in shade.

Does raw Indian hair come in jet black?

Some natural hair looks very dark, but a uniform blue-black or jet-black finish may be the result of coloring. The label “raw” is used differently by sellers; if unprocessed hair is important to you, ask whether the hair has been dyed, bleached, coated or otherwise treated.

Why does raw Indian hair look brown in sunlight?

Sunlight can reveal warm undertones that are less visible indoors. Camera exposure and white balance may exaggerate this effect. Compare photos taken in natural indirect light, direct sunlight and indoor lighting before deciding that a bundle is a different shade.

How to match raw Indian hair to your own hair

Start with an unfiltered photograph of your hair in daylight. Compare undertones as well as darkness. Request photos of the exact bundle or batch when possible, and check the seller’s exchange policy before making a color-sensitive purchase.

Can you dye raw Indian hair?

Human hair can often be colored, but results depend on its previous processing, porosity and condition. Lightening can cause damage, and there is no guarantee every bundle will lift evenly. A strand test and professional colorist are sensible precautions.

What to ask a hair supplier about color

Ask for the exact bundle photographed under consistent lighting, disclosure of any dye or bleach, whether the bundle is naturally dark or color-treated, and how much color variation to expect between bundles.

The Science Behind Natural Hair Colour

Two pigments account for every natural human hair colour that exists. According to the National Institutes of Health, eumelanin produces black and brown tones, while pheomelanin produces red and yellow tones, and nearly all human hair contains some mix of both. The ratio between the two, not a single fixed pigment, is what creates the entire natural colour spectrum from platinum blonde to jet black.

Peer-reviewed chemical analysis of human hair pigmentation has confirmed that eumelanin-dominant hair reads darker and cooler, while a higher relative share of pheomelanin shifts hair toward red and auburn tones, even within hair that looks similarly dark at a glance. This is why two donors can both be classified as "black hair" and still produce visibly different results once light hits the strand at an angle.

Raw Indian hair typically carries a high concentration of eumelanin with very little pheomelanin, which explains the deep, slightly warm dark shades on this chart. Research on melanin regulation in hair follicles also confirms that this pigment ratio is set at the follicle level and does not change after the hair leaves the scalp, which is part of why authentic raw hair colour stays stable through washing.

Why Raw Indian Hair Is Rarely True Jet Black

Jet black, sometimes labeled shade , is a cosmetic colour. It is nearly impossible to find naturally in large quantities from a single donor. Most "jet black" bundles on the market have been artificially darkened during processing.

Factories that darken commercial hair are chasing a look, not biology. They dye batches uniformly so every bundle photographs the same. Real donor hair does not work that way, and spectrophotometric studies of hair pigmentation show that natural eumelanin-rich strands reflect light unevenly compared to synthetic black dye, which is part of why dyed hair often looks flatter under direct light. That natural unevenness is a feature, not a flaw.

Why Your Raw Indian Hair Looks Brown in Sunlight

This is one of the most common questions salon buyers and individual customers ask after their first wash.

Indoor lighting is usually warmer and dimmer than daylight, which makes dark hair read as flatter and blacker. Step outside, and UV-rich sunlight interacts with the eumelanin in the hair shaft, revealing the natural brown undertone that was there all along. The American Academy of Dermatology notes that sun exposure affects the look and integrity of hair fibers directly, which is part of why colourists expect some visible shift between indoor and outdoor lighting on natural, unprocessed hair.

Artificially darkened hair does not do this the same way. Heavy dye coats the outer cuticle instead of reflecting light from within the strand, so it tends to look flat both indoors and out. If your bundle shifts colour in sunlight, that shift is usually a sign you bought genuinely raw, single-donor hair.

Prolonged, repeated sun exposure can also gradually lighten and warm the ends of any hair, extensions included, which is why the AAD recommends UV protection for hair the same way it recommends it for skin, especially in strong-sun climates.6

How to Read a Bundle's True Natural Colour Before You Buy

Reading raw hair colour correctly takes more than a glance at a product photo, especially when photos are taken under studio lighting.

Check the listing for "single donor" language. True single-donor bundles will not promise perfectly uniform colour across a full head, since that uniformity usually signals blending or dye correction.

Ask for a photo in natural daylight. A supplier confident in their sourcing will happily show a bundle outdoors, not just under studio soft boxes.

Common Mistakes Buyers Make When Matching Colour

Assuming "natural black" always means the same shade. Two suppliers can both label a product "natural black" while selling completely different actual tones, since the term is not standardized industry-wide, and the FDA notes that most hair color products are not required to be pre-approved before sale, which leaves labeling language largely up to the manufacturer.7

Judging colour only from a phone photo. Screen colour calibration and studio lighting can shift a dark brown bundle to look pure black in an image.

Panicking when bundles from the same order look slightly different. This is expected with single-donor hair and is not the same as a quality defect.

Assuming color variation means the hair is fake or synthetic. The opposite is usually true. Perfectly matched, uniform color across every bundle is more often a sign of heavy processing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Natural 1B the same as jet black? No. Natural 1B is a softer, deeper off-black with visible warmth and depth, while jet black is a flat, cosmetically uniform shade rarely found naturally in bulk.

Why do bundles from the same order look slightly different colors? Because raw Indian hair is single-donor and unprocessed, small natural variations in pigment are normal and expected, not a sign of poor quality.

Can raw Indian hair be naturally blonde or red? No. Authentic raw Indian temple hair does not naturally grow in blonde, red, or copper tones. Any hair in those shades has been chemically processed.

Does color variation affect how well the hair blends with my own? Usually it helps rather than hurts, since real human hair rarely grows in one flat, uniform shade either.

How can I tell if "natural black" hair has been dyed? Check for a strong chemical smell, unusually high shine, and a color that looks identical indoors and outdoors. Genuine raw hair shifts slightly in sunlight.

Does sun exposure permanently change the color of raw Indian hair? Repeated, prolonged sun exposure can gradually warm and lighten the ends over time, similar to how natural hair weathers outdoors. Limiting direct sun exposure and using UV-protective leave-in products slows this shift.

The Bottom Line

A real raw Indian hair color chart is not one flat black square. It is a narrow, believable spectrum from soft off-black to warm dark brown, shaped by biology rather than a dye lot. That natural variation is exactly what makes single-donor, temple-sourced hair blend so convincingly with real human hair.
