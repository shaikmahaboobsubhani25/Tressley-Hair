What Australian Salons Should Check Before Ordering From India

A supplier can look professional online and still be the wrong fit for your salon.

Start with the hair itself.

Ask whether the product is raw, virgin or otherwise processed. Ask whether the hair comes from one donor or multiple donors.

Then request a sample.

Install the sample using the same method your salon uses for clients. Wash it, dry it and inspect shedding and tangling.

For raw hair, natural texture variation is not automatically a defect. Your clients may actually prefer a texture that looks less uniform.

Next, check the commercial side.

Ask for the MOQ, price per bundle or gram, production time, shipping cost and replacement policy.

Also ask what happens if the next batch does not match your approved sample.

Tressley's current shipping policy is a useful example of why you should separate processing time from shipping time. Its policy states that in-stock orders can be processed within 24 to 72 hours, while custom textures require additional processing.

What About GST and Import Costs in Australia?

Do not compare Indian supplier prices alone.

Your real cost is the landed cost.

The Australian Border Force states that GST is generally payable on taxable imported goods. The standard GST rate on taxable importations is 10 percent.

For higher-value imports, Australia also applies import declaration and border requirements.

Australian Border Force states that goods valued above AUD 1,000 generally require an import declaration and payment of applicable taxes and charges at the border.

That means your buying calculation should include:

  • Hair cost
  • International freight
  • Insurance
  • GST
  • Customs charges where applicable
  • Broker or clearance fees
  • Local delivery
  • Packaging or private-label costs

For a salon, this calculation is more useful than comparing the advertised bundle price.

Two suppliers can both advertise Indian hair while offering very different products.

Raw Indian hair normally refers to hair with minimal or no processing after collection. The exact definition varies across sellers, so you should ask what they mean by raw.

How Australian Salons Can Test a New Indian Supplier

Order one sample first.

Record the supplier name, product name, length, weight and texture.

Photograph the bundle when it arrives.

Then test it under normal salon conditions.

Wash the hair using your normal professional routine. Let it dry naturally. Check whether the texture changes.

Test the weft or attachment area for loose strands.

For clip-ins, inspect clips and stitching.

For tape-ins, check the attachment quality and adhesive performance.

For closures and frontals, inspect lace quality and hairline construction.

The goal is not to prove that a supplier is good after one test.

The goal is to find out whether the product matches the specification you were promised.

After approval, ask the supplier to keep the same specification for future orders.

Common Mistakes Australian Salon Owners Make

Choosing the cheapest supplier

A lower bundle price can disappear after freight, GST, replacement costs and inconsistent batches.

Ordering bulk before testing

A 20-kilogram order is not a sample.

Test first, then scale.

Assuming every "raw" product is identical

Raw hair can vary naturally by donor, texture and density.

Ignoring customs responsibility

Ask who pays import taxes and who handles customs clearance.

Failing to document an approved sample

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best Indian hair supplier for Australian salons?

There is no universal best supplier. Tressley Hair, IDM Hair, Adorable Raw Indian Hair, Varshini Hair Impex and other suppliers in this guide are worth comparing based on product fit, samples, MOQ and landed cost.

Can Australian salons buy raw Indian hair directly from India?

Yes. Australian salons can source directly from Indian suppliers, but they must account for import requirements, GST, freight and customs charges.

How much hair should a salon order first?

Start with samples or a small professional order. The correct quantity depends on your installation method, client demand and the supplier's MOQ.

Should I buy bundles or ready-made extensions?

Choose based on your service menu. Bundles work well for sew-ins and custom wig production, while clip-ins, tape-ins and other formats suit specific installation services.

How do I verify a genuine Indian hair supplier?

Ask for business details, sourcing information, export documentation, physical samples and clear commercial terms. India's DGFT provides an online facility for checking IEC information.

What should I ask an Indian hair supplier before ordering?

Ask about origin, donor type, processing, weight, length, texture, MOQ, price, sample policy, production time, shipping, customs documents and returns.

Does Indian hair work well for Australian salon clients?

Indian human hair is used internationally for extensions, wigs and professional styling. The right choice depends on the client's texture, installation method, colour requirements and maintenance routine.
